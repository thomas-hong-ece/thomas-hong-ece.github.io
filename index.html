PROJECT LOG
LOG 01 · FIELD TEST · 2ND PLACE, AEP DYNAMIC GRID CHALLENGE

Dynamic Transmission Line Monitor

24-hour makeathon build (MakeOHI/O 2026): a live demonstration of dynamic line rating. Nichrome wire stands in for a conductor; an ESP32 reads contact temperature, ambient conditions, and a time-of-flight LiDAR tracking sag off a weighted target. As the wire heats, it expands, and the system watches it happen.

The hard part was physical: every sensor mount melted off past 50 °C until heat-shrink tubing held at 4am. Logged 700+ measurements; analysis matched IEEE 738 thermal expansion models within ~5% error, including a forced-convection cooling demo most teams didn't model.

LOG 04 · DESIGN REVIEW · CITED AS MODEL FOR FUTURE REVIEWS

IgnisKey: Custom Heated Key Switch · Boeing-Sponsored Internship

A heated keyboard needs its element close enough to the touch surface to matter, and off-the-shelf switches have no volume to put one in. So the switch became the design problem. I packaged a thermistor into a custom two-piece housing, seated above the PCB in the switch cavity, with the heat path running up through the stem into a carbon fiber keycap. Carbon fiber conducts poorly through its thickness but well in-plane, so heat arriving at one small stem contact spreads across the whole key instead of pooling.

Led a 4-person team through 5 prototype iterations, balancing thermal against ergonomic constraints and cutting build complexity each revision. Delivered a functional prototype and a formal design review to Boeing engineers on schedule. Our trade-off documentation was cited by their engineers as a model for future reviews.

Bench: mechanical design (Onshape) · PCB design · thermal packaging · materials selection · design review · team lead of 4
LOG 05 · IN DEVELOPMENT · ADD ORDER DECODE PASSING, 1-CYCLE LATENCY

FPGA Market-Data Decoder (Verilog)

An ITCH-style exchange-feed decoder: a byte-serial finite state machine that parses the 36-byte Add Order message, a trigger module doing a parallel compare against a configurable watchlist, and a self-checking testbench driven by a Python packet generator. Success metric is clock cycles from last byte in to valid out, currently 1, deterministic across every vector.

The generator emits six deliberate corner cases before any random ones: all zeros, all ones, single bit set, high bit only, and payload bytes that collide with the message-type marker. Uniform random values essentially never reach those boundaries, and they are where shift-register bugs actually live. Add Order is verified; other message types are not written yet, and the repo says so.
